# History of Srokeskia

## Prehistory ##

Srokeskia was originally populated in the Upper Paleolithic by a population of hunter gathers that appear to have originated from either Australia or Sundaland, though most likely the former. This population appears to have been chiefly of Australian Aboriginal origin, but with significant admixture from other melanesian groups, and a population related to the ancestors of the Jomon and other ancient North East Asian Groups. They also appear to have mixed with archaic hominins, both Denisovans and an unknown species, before reaching Huluria.

| Percentage Contributed | Ancestral Population |
|---|---|
| ~20% | Papuan-Melanesian |
| ~60% | Australian-Aboriginal–like ancestry (primary) |
| ~12% | Ancient East Asian Jomon-like group |
| ~13% | Denisovan ancestry |
| ~2% | Neanderthal |
| ~0.5% | unknown Homo sp. |

The Neanderthal ancestry and about half of the Denisovan ancestry was inherited from their ancestral Homo sapiens populations, but the rest of their archaic ancestry they gained after their population was isolated.

-----------

This population lived in isolation in Srokeskia for the rest of the Paleolithic, living a Hunter-Gatherer lifestyle and hunting the abundant megafauna on the island. They adapted to their new island home, both culturally and physically, dividing into three main groups, the west coast (OWC), east coast (OEC), and plateau (OH). As the Pleistocene transitioned into the Holocene, and the glaciation at high elevations subsided, These three groups expanded into five. The west coast group retreated further south, being partially replaced in the north by a group from the east coast. The east coast divided into the northern group, and the main east coast group (which received admixture from the plateau). A distinct mountain population formed as well, a mixture of the more highland dwelling west coast population, and a westward migration from the plateau. Thus, the indigenous population at 6000 BC can be divided as this.

| Population | Abreviation | Ancestral Population Proportions
|---|---|---|
| Ancient Western Coastal Dwellers | AWCD | nearly 100% descended from the OWC |
| Highland Hunter Gatherers | HHG | 60% OWC, 40% OH |
| Plateau Hunter Gatherers | PHG | 95% OH, 5% OWC |
| Eastern Lowland Generalists | ELG | 70% OEC, 15% OH, 5% OWC |
| Northern Hunter Gatherers | NHG | 50% OEC, 30% OWC, 20% OH |

At approximately this time, a small population of mixed Nilotic and Eastern Hunter Gatherer ancestry arrived on the east coast. This migration is associated with the Dzemali, and though this population was not large, it was able to replace a large portion of the AWCD, and constituted about 30% of the resulting population. Some groups of AWCD migrated into the lower ranges of mountains nearer the coast, where they persisted for a time and mixed with the local highland population. Thus, the western populations of HHG had an increased amount of OWC ancestry when compared to more eastern populations.

## The Advent of Agriculture ##

Around 5000 BC, a group of Neolithic Farmers from the south of India arrived in Srokeskia. While it is unclear whether they introduced agriculture, or whether it had already begun to be experimented with, it is certain that they were the group that introduced some of the most important crops and livestock to Srokeskia. Wheat, barley, flax, sheep, goats, cattle, and pigs all first arrived with these people. They made a sizable ancestral input on Northern and Eastern Srokeskia, especially agriculturalists. 

After their arrival, many groups in the north of Srokeskia, and also the East, began to adopt agriculture. They settled down in villages, with some of the largest ones reaching a few hundred strong. They grew wheat, barley, komos, and flax, and kept herds of pigs, sheep, goats, and cattle. The tropical Zebu cattle did not fare too well in all but the far northern regions of Huluria, and neither did the tropical hogs, but other than that, animal husbandry was a resounding success. It was adopted wholeheartedly by the people of the plateau, who partially abandoned their traditional hunting and gathering lifestyle to be nomadic pastoralists. They also  domesticated a local camelid, a local wild goat/antelope, and a local type of deer.

--------------

At about the year 900 BC, a new wave of migration from Australia arrived. A Pama Nyungan speaking group of warriors found their way to Huluria, and settled on the Black Islands, an island chain off the east coast. They established themselves there, many of them took wives of the previous inhabitants of the island or of neighboring peoples, and they began to build villages and farm the land. A couple of centuries later, they exploded outwards, conquering the rest of the south eastern regions, and reaching as far north as the modern city of Simperu. They established a stratified, centrally organized kingdom and developed a sophisticated culture. They invented a writing system, and learned how to work bronze out of copper and both tin and arsenic. They did not become a truly bronze age civilization, but rather used them to a similar extent to metalworking societies in the Americas. They built a capital city called Krayta Wratka, on the coast north of where the modern city of Sônargãw lies.

This was the origin of the Jure civilization. Their kingdom at its peak boasted more than 80,000 inhabitants, and they were able to project power far beyond their core regions in modern day Idrims, Gunam, and Maucāka. They campaigned in the north, subjugating many of the farming polities there. They stretched their power into the plateau, building forward bases and towns in the Šroké valley, and building fortifications along the border between the plateau and the lowlands to guard themselves against raiders. Coastal raids from the Jure even managed to trouble the distant Dzemali, and a few expeditions and explorers found their way into the high interior mountain ranges. Although much of what they built has been destroyed or fallen into ruin, a few documents have persisted to tell us their history, and legends of their splendor are still told all over Srokeskia. 

One of their most important sites is Gernalngi. This is a small Jure town, seemingly the site of a summer palace for the uppermost of upper crust, that was buried in a volcanic ash during the 213 BC eruption of Mount Bluehead. Though few bodies were deposited within the town, many paintings, inscriptions, carvings, and everyday items were perfectly preserved. This discovery gives us much insight into the lives and culture of the hight of the Jure. Unfortunately, that golden age was about to come to an end. The volcanic eruption that led to famine, political strife, economic upheaval, and the collapse of the classical Jure was the same eruption that so carefully preserved artifacts of it. The Jure kingdom and civilization would continue on, it would never be as strong or as united. Surviving documents tell of civil war, breakaway states, and peasant/tribal uprisings. The bureaucracy was understaffed and overstretched, the army was weakened and mutinous, and the provinces and vassals were disenchanted and rebellious. Estimates say that up to 30,000 died or were displaced in the famine and strife that followed, and many of the hardest hit regions were in the core regions of the Jure realm, in Maucāka. The Jure would be relatively easy pickings for what came next.

Just before this time of troubles, or so legends say, a boy named Merroney was born. Although primary sources are absent, it seems that when Merroney saw the destruction around him, and concluded that it must be punishment from a spiritual or devine source for an inbalance in the world. He believed that balance came from a respect all things have for each other, likening it to the water in a pond, which all the pond critters need to support themselves. He believed that if the world continued the way it was going, humanity would be destroyed, and a second creation would take place. Thus, he saw it as the duty of every person to live by respect, and to cleanse society of all injustice. He believed that could be achieved by the destruction of the unjust. He believed that the large, centralized temples of the Jure civilization needed to be destroyed, because if there was a moral failing on the part of the people managing them, they would bring destruction on the entire land. On the contrary, he believed that every person within the Jure lands needed to adhere to the religion of the Jure people faithfully, and anyone who didn't could not be tolerated. Merroney's ideas spread like wildfire. They gave people something and someone to blame for the destruction, the moral failings of the elites and the rest of society. Ruling powers feared this movement and tried to stamp it out, but they failed. They even tried to kill the originator of the movement himself, but it appears that he fled into the wilderness where he survived and attracted a following, which he taught and from which came the later leaders of the movement. Eventually, a large scale peasant revolt sprang up around this movement, and Merroney attempted to lead his following forth, to raise an army and take control of the movement, but he was intercepted. Some tales say that it was due to treachery, but others say that he was betrayed. Regardless, his followers were crushed, and he was caught while trying to flee, captured, and brutally executed. His ideas did not die with him, but eventually the rebellions were mostly put down, and any real chance of his followers taking control of the Jure kingdom was quashed.

His ideas are considered the first major contributor to the Srokeskian ideal of Janedkora, a system of societal order where peace and prosperity are maintained by mutual bonds of respect. Though most modern scholars believe that those ideas are older than Merroney, he is the first recorded thinker to think this way. Most of his ideas and methods were unique to the Jure society, but the basic tenents of it, the way he likened the world to a pond and bonds of respect to the water, would spread over the coming centuries. influencing the traditional thought, values, and belief systems up and down Srokeskia. The first major spread of Merroneyan thought out of the Jure lands appears to have come at the hands of refugees fleeing the next major upheaval, the rise of the Sea-Folk.

## Warriors from the Waves ##

In 120 BC, a new group of people arrived from the sea in the northern part of Srokeskia, in Osmeria. These people were know as the Sea Folk, they were vicious warriors who knew how to work iron, and were the first Iron age civilization in Srokeskia. Archeological and linguistic evidence pointsto an origin in southern Iran, with possible roots in Elymais or Balochistan. They appear to have had two languages, and Iranian one and an Elamite one. They brought the first horses to Huluria, and military technology that was hard for any native group to match.

Their first king, Ulankol, spent his reign attempting to build a stable realm for his people After establishing his rule on the north shore of Gunam bay, he died. Under the reign of his two sons, the Sea Folk quickly began to take advantage of the weakened and decentralized state the land was in. They were easily able to overwhelm the Jure, Conquering Gunam by 98 BC, Indrims by 97 BC, and took control of Krayta Wratka in the same year. And just like that, the unending Jure kingdom was conquered. For the most part, the Sea Folk were able to merely take over the governing aparatus of the Jure, but in the far south, in and around Lossia and the Šroké Delta, as well as the Black Islands, Jure rump states still persisted.

The next two decades were spent consolidating. One of brothers, the official king, spent his time campainging, mopping up Jure remnants and breakaway rebellions, and established Ulankolid dominion over Easter Srokeskia. The other brother oraginized and consolidated control of the country, establishing the old Jure upper and administrative classes as the new bureaucracy. By 60 BC, almost all of the old Jure lands were solidly under the control of the Ulankolids, except for some minor kingdoms in the far south of Lossia, around the Šroke Delta and the rugged terrain seen around Fristhawy Bay. Even the proud Plateau tribes had been decimated by the war, and forced to acknowledge the Ulankolid Regime's overlordship.

The Ulankolids did not have complete and utter dominance over Srokestkia however. In some of the more elevated and rugged portions of the plateau, independent minded tribes refused the imperial yoke. The mountains in the west were too rugged to conquer, and not worth the fight. Some polities in the far north or far south only paid lip service to imperial dominance.
