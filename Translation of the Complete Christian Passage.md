# Translation of the Complete Christian Passage (Section 7.4)

## Full Source Text

> **[1]** Jeswe un kmit o jekto ", skjo dajkê, a njet majnsers ktats sinatlo kwom u na?"
>
> **[2]** Kjan un it ",kno du was Jawjos Anni doms kolkmat mandoms sajtô mandoms dlirsko. Je wu jims man mejns ktats sinatlo. Wins ihu. Kno du was o lwosk musk, kmustwe seucisu was kno u na. Biswam u jik bjats nejt wins na.
>
> **[3]** Re jestam fu bfolo stu jomad os stjeki Annjot. Kmum face jom, jemu alkisuj sbary os lo ot was Annjot. Je,i du tekwot e nanrohkeldij id bfolo, jemu estam du kimtjekki os itja ad bfolol. O itja na u mejma kmu mejma o face jestam aus isko estam manaus jakôs estam. Jom fun homad objos lo ot was Annjot a nanrohkeldi na.
>
> **[4]** Jom kmedu face ihi kelî isfun askit, na stusu kbil. Ru na stu lûska Annjos, bisje kjan u jeus â rohekba, na isu mejma bjetoms aswotni dmo kinast stu kbilr e siko aswotnje.
>
> **[5]** Jeswe o josu Jawjot maneus isko un kitaj isuj fristo manisuj bliran aus sbarî. Anni u was u stofka u klowot u ferotska brekt, os osu ji kjans mjeum enhjoki mjeum facem kjan un busk, o was tsane mjeu mjema. Jeswos kmufu fir aute jitja jomê. Na fu mjema au iskoje isu stibje kinast stum face bitu knembon stambwan os jak. Na u swe a deje Annjot. O iskoje isu riwaj isu dokol isu sot Jeswe kmum kbil esos lo ot was Annjot, na u swa o loj ot jak.
>
> **[6]** Os bejus musk tsu numindeu hbajkte janen, eu kujka jestam manau bretmej estam ikwu sjotam es dojna mrojd.
>
> **[7]** Jeswos fun tjalm na mukwo, was iskmu eswe os kalbo u na.

## Translation

### [1] The Question

> *Jeswe un kmit o jekto ", skjo dajkê, a njet majnsers ktats sinatlo kwom u na?"*

**Jeswe asked the teacher: "O wise one, what is the most important directive in the Law?"**

### [2] The Greatest Commandment

> *Kjan un it ",kno du was Jawjos Anni doms kolkmat mandoms sajtô mandoms dlirsko. Je wu jims man mejns ktats sinatlo. Wins ihu. Kno du was o lwosk musk, kmustwe seucisu was kno u na. Biswam u jik bjats nejt wins na.*

**He spoke: "You must love Jawjos God with your heart, and with your mind, and with your human spirit. This is the first and most important in the Law. The second is like it: you must love your neighbor as yourself. On these two all the directives stand."**

### [3] The Fall — Sin, Inherited Guilt, and Exile from God's Pasture

> *Re jestam fu bfolo stu jomad os stjeki Annjot.*

**For everyone has transgressed and does not attain the perfection of God.**

> *Kmum face jom, jemu alkisuj sbary os lo ot was Annjot.*

**We will certainly die — we who are wandering aimlessly from the pasture of God's love.**

> *Je,i du tekwot e nanrohkeldij id bfolo, jemu estam du kimtjekki os itja ad bfolol.*

*je* "this" + *i* (inan. indef. nom.) + *du* "must" + *tekwot* "to pass on, to inherit" + *e* (anim. indef.) + *nanrohkeldij* (*nanrohkeldi* "tendency" in some case form) + *id* (inan. indef. gen.) + *bfolo* "transgression"; *jemu* "we" (erg.) + *estam* "all" + *du* "must" + *kimtjekki* "to carry something heavy" + *os* (def. anim. acc.) + *itja* "inherited responsibility" + *ad* (def. inan. gen.) + *bfolol* (*bfolo* with some suffix)

**This, a tendency toward transgression, must be passed on; we all must carry the heavy [burden of] the inherited responsibility of transgression.**

> *O itja na u mejma kmu mejma o face jestam aus isko estam manaus jakôs estam.*

*o* (def. anim. nom.) + *itja* "inherited responsibility" + *na* "what" (relativizer) + *u* (pres.) + *mejma* "cause" + *kmu* (*km-* "certainly" + *u* pres.) + *mejma* "cause" + *o* (def. anim.) + *face* "death/to die" + *jestam* (*j-* + *estam* "all") + *aus* (def. pl. anim. dat.) + *isko* "person" + *estam* "all" + *manaus* (*man* "and" + *aus*) + *jakôs* "living things/creatures" + *estam* "all"

**The inherited responsibility that causes — certainly causes — the death of all people and all creatures.**

> *Jom fun homad objos lo ot was Annjot a nanrohkeldi na.*

*jom* "we" (abs.) + *fun* (perf. past) + *homad* (*homan* "pull" in some form) + *obj-* "out of" + *os* (def. anim. acc./dat.) + *lo* "pasture/domain" + *ot* (def. anim. gen.) + *was* "love" + *Annjot* "of God" + *a* (def. inan. nom.) + *nanrohkeldi* "tendency" + *na* (relativizer)

**We have been pulled out of the pasture of God's love — by that tendency.**

### [4] The Impossibility of Self-Rescue

> *Jom kmedu face ihi kelî isfun askit, na stusu kbil.*

*jom* "we" + *kmedu* (*km-* "certainly" + *ed-*? or *d-* "must" — possibly "certainly must") + *face* "die" + *ihi* (*ih-* "as/like" + *i* — "like") + *kelî* "water" + *isfun* (*is-* participial + *fun* perf. past — "that has been") + *askit* "spilled" + *na* "what/which" + *stusu* (*st-* negation + *u* + *su*? — "cannot") + *kbil* "recover/regain"

**We will certainly die like water that has been spilled, which cannot be recovered.**

> *Ru na stu lûska Annjos, bisje kjan u jeus â rohekba, na isu mejma bjetoms aswotni dmo kinast stu kbilr e siko aswotnje.*

*ru* "but" + *na* "what" + *stu* (neg.) + *lûska* "want/desire" + *Annjos* (*Anni* "God" + *-os* proper noun erg.) — "but God does not want"; *bisje* (*bj-* "on/upon" + *sje*? — possibly "rather, instead") + *kjan* "he" + *u* (pres.) + *jeus* (unknown — possibly *je* "this" + *eus* "to form/fix/prepare") + *â* (def. pl. inan. acc.) + *rohekba* "paths/ways/habits"; *na* + *isu* (participial pres.) + *mejma* "causing" + *bjetoms* (*bje* "think" + *-toms* — "thoughts about?" or *dom-* "about" rearranged) + *aswotni* (*astwotni* "banishment" variant) + *dmo* (*dm-* "apart from" + *o* def. anim.) + *kinast* "him" (3sg abs.) + *stu* (neg.) + *kbilr* (*kbil* "recover" + *-r* — "not recovering" or comparative) + *e* (indef. anim.) + *siko* "person" + *aswotnje* (*astwotni* + adj. ending — "banished")

**But that is not what God wants; rather, he prepares the paths [back], which cause thoughts of banishment apart from him — [so that] a banished person might not fail to recover.**

### [5] The Gospel — God Sends His Son

> *Jeswe o josu Jawjot maneus isko un kitaj isuj fristo manisuj bliran aus sbarî.*

*Jeswe* (proper name) + *o* (def. anim. nom.) + *josu* (*osu* "child/offspring" with *j-*? — "son/child of") + *Jawjot* (*Jawjos* + *-ot* gen. — "of Jawjos/God") + *maneus* (*man* "and" + *eus* indef. pl. anim. acc. — "and [for]") + *isko* "person/people" + *un* (past) + *kitaj* "come" + *isuj* (*is-* participial + *uj* continuous — "one who is [doing]") + *fristo* "hunting/seeking/pursuing" + *manisuj* (*man* + *isuj* — "and one who is") + *bliran* "rescuing/saving" + *aus* (def. pl. anim. dat.) + *sbarî* (*sbari* "wanderers" + circumflex — "those who are wandering")

**Jeswe, the child of Jawjos, and [for] all people, came — one who is seeking and one who is saving those who are wandering.**

> *Anni u was u stofka u klowot u ferotska brekt, os osu ji kjans mjeum enhjoki mjeum facem kjan un busk, o was tsane mjeu mjema.*

*Anni* "God" + *u was u stofka u klowot u ferotska* (pres. + four love verbs — "loves, likes, cares for, and deeply loves") + *brekt* (*breket* "of great quantity/number" — "greatly/abundantly"); *os* (def. anim. acc.) + *osu* "child" + *ji* "one" + *kjans* (3sg gen. "his") + *mjeum* (*mje-* "thus/with purpose of" + *um* future — "so that [in future]") + *enhjoki* (*en-* "great" + *hjoki* "hurt/pain" — "great suffering") + *mjeum* "and so that" + *facem* (*face* "die" + *-m*? — "death/dying") + *kjan* "he" + *un* (past) + *busk* "sent"; *o* (def. anim.) + *was* "love" + *tsane* "enough/sufficient" + *mjeu* (*mje-* "thus" + *u* pres.) + *mejma* "cause"

**God loves, likes, cares for, and deeply cherishes [us] abundantly — his one child, so that through great suffering and through death, he sent [him]; love sufficient thus to be the cause.**

> *Jeswos kmufu fir aute jitja jomê.*

*Jeswos* (erg. form of Jeswe) + *kmufu* (*km-* "certainly" + *u* + *f-* perf. — "has certainly") + *fir* "seized/taken hold of" + *aute* (def. pl. anim. gen. — "of the") + *jitja* (*jita* "responsibility/duty" — variant or modified form) + *jomê* (1pl gen. "our")

**Jeswe has certainly seized [upon himself] all of our responsibilities/duties.**

> *Na fu mjema au iskoje isu stibje kinast stum face bitu knembon stambwan os jak.*

*na* "what/which" + *fu* (perf. pres.) + *mjema* "caused" + *au* (def. pl. anim.) + *iskoje* "the ones who" + *isu* (participial) + *stibje* "believe" + *kinast* "him" (3sg abs.) + *stum* (*st-* neg. + *um* future — "will not") + *face* "die" + *bitu* (*bit-* "rather" + *u* pres.) + *knembon* "to possess/have" + *stambwan* "forever" + *os* (def. anim. acc.) + *jak* "life"

**Which has caused [that] the ones who believe in him will not die, but rather possess life forever.**

> *Na u swe a deje Annjot.*

*na* "what" + *u* (pres.) + *swe* (unknown — possibly *eswe* "substance/material" or a copular/existential form) + *a* (def. inan. nom.) + *deje* "gift/give" + *Annjot* "of God"

**That is the gift of God.**

> *O iskoje isu riwaj isu dokol isu sot Jeswe kmum kbil esos lo ot was Annjot, na u swa o loj ot jak.*

*o* (def. anim.) + *iskoje* "the one who" + *isu* (part.) + *riwaj* (*irwaj* "to know someone" — variant with metathesis) + *isu dokol* "trusts" + *isu sot* "follows" + *Jeswe* — "the one who knows, trusts, and follows Jeswe"; *kmum* "will certainly" + *kbil* "recover/return" + *esos* (*es-* "to/toward" + *os* def. anim. acc.) + *lo* "pasture" + *ot was Annjot* "of the love of God"; *na* + *u swa* (pres. + unknown — possibly "which is") + *o loj* (*lo* "pasture" + *-j*? — "the pasture/domain") + *ot jak* "of life"

**The one who knows, trusts, and follows Jeswe will certainly return to the pasture of God's love — which is the pasture of life.**

### [6] The Commission

> *Os bejus musk tsu numindeu hbajkte janen, eu kujka jestam manau bretmej estam ikwu sjotam es dojna mrojd.*

*os* (def. anim. acc.) + *bejus* "creation/what is created" + *musk* "your" (sg. gen.) + *tsu* (imperative particle) + *numindeu* (*numind-* "via/by way of" + *eu* indef. pl. anim.) + *hbajkte* (*hbaj-* "during/while" + *kt-* "in/within" + *e* indef. anim.? — "while among") + *janen* (*jan* "youth" + *-en* "great" — "the younger generation" or "the youths"); *eu* (indef. pl. anim.) + *kujka* "the things of life, events of life and history" + *jestam* "all" + *manau* (*man* "and" + *au* def. pl. anim.) + *bretmej* "seasons" + *estam* "all" + *ikwu* (*ikw-* "before in time" + *u* pres.) + *sjotam* "lost/fallen short of" + *es* (*es-* "to/toward") + *dojna* "flavor" + *mrojd* (inan. pl. gen. "of them")

**Your creation — by way of [all] peoples, while among the youth — all the things of life and all the seasons, before they are lost, to the flavor of them [all]: go!**

Smoother: **Go, by way of all peoples, and while among the young, [tell them of] all of life's events and all its seasons, before they are lost to the taste of them.**

### [7] The Closing

> *Jeswos fun tjalm na mukwo, was iskmu eswe os kalbo u na.*

*Jeswos* (erg.) + *fun* (perf. past) + *tjalm* (*tjelm* "to teach/tell a story" — variant) + *na* "what" + *mukwo* (2sg dat. "to you"); *was* "love" + *iskmu* (*isko* "person" + possessive? or *is-* part. + *kmu* "certainly" — uncertain) + *eswe* "to be formed out of / substance" + *os* (def. anim. acc.) + *kalbo* (*kalboj/kalbwo* "to raise up for another's benefit / self-sacrifice") + *u na* (pres. + relativizer)

**Jeswe has taught you what love is made of — what self-sacrifice is.**

>

## Biblical Identification

The passage is a Ferrestan **catechetical summary** — not a direct translation of a single Biblical chapter but a structured theological composition drawing from multiple sources:

| Section | Biblical Source |
|---|---|
| [1]–[2] The Greatest Commandment | Matthew 22:36–40 / Mark 12:28–31 |
| [3] Universal sin and its consequences | Romans 3:23, Romans 5:12, Romans 6:23a |
| [4] "Like water spilled" / God's desire to save | 2 Samuel 14:14 (almost verbatim) |
| [5] God sends his son; eternal life through belief | John 3:16–17; Romans 5:8; Romans 6:23b |
| [6] The Great Commission | Matthew 28:19–20 (heavily adapted) |
| [7] Closing summary | 1 John 3:16 (paraphrased) |

The 2 Samuel 14:14 allusion — "we will certainly die like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be recovered" — is an especially striking choice. This verse, spoken by the wise woman of Tekoa to King David, is not commonly used in catechisms. Its inclusion here suggests either a translator with deep biblical literacy, or a Ferrestan theological tradition that independently found this metaphor compelling.

## What the Passage Reveals About Ferrestan Culture

### 1. Sin as Boundary-Breaking, Not Moral Stain

The passage consistently uses *bfolo* ("crime, breach of boundaries, breach of trust") for sin. This is not a translation of the Greek *hamartia* ("missing the mark") or the Hebrew *het* ("missing/falling short"). It is the Ferrestan legal term for boundary violation — the same word used for theft, murder, and embezzlement in the Ferrestan legal code. The Ferrestan concept of sin is **structural and communal**: you have crossed a line that damages the fabric of relationships, exactly as a *bfolo* damages clan relations.

### 2. Sin as Inherited Collective Burden

The phrase *os itja ad bfolol* — "the inherited responsibility of transgression" — uses *itja*, the Ferrestan concept of reputation so powerful it passes to descendants. This is not merely "original sin" in the Western theological sense. It is the Ferrestan cultural concept of *itja* applied to the primordial transgression: just as a clan member's great deed or great shame follows their descendants, the first *bfolo* follows all humanity. The verb *kimtjekki* ("to carry something heavy") makes the weight physical and tangible — this is a burden you carry on your back, not an abstract legal status.

### 3. Death as Wandering from the Herd's Pasture / Spilled Water

Two metaphors for death:

- **Wandering aimlessly (*sbary*) from the pasture (*lo*) of God's love.** As analyzed before, this casts spiritual death as a stray animal drifting from its clan's territory — a concrete image in a herding culture.

- **Water spilled on the ground (*kelî isfun askit*) that cannot be recovered (*stusu kbil*).** This is the 2 Samuel 14:14 image, and it must have resonated deeply with a mountain people who live in a landscape where water is channeled, stored, and rationed through seasons. Spilled water in a mountain village is gone — it runs downhill, absorbed by the earth, irrecoverable. The choice of this metaphor (over other available biblical images of death) reveals a culture that understands scarcity and irreversibility viscerally.

### 4. God's Love Expressed Through Five Distinct Verbs

The passage describes God's love with four sequential verbs: *u was u stofka u klowot u ferotska* — "loves, likes, cares-for, and deeply-cherishes." This is not repetition for emphasis. These are four distinct Ferrestan words for different kinds and intensities of affection:

- *was* — to love, care for, nourish (active caregiving)
- *stofka* — to like, to have affection for (warm personal regard)
- *klowot* — to love in a less intimate way, to like very much (broad deep fondness)
- *ferotska* — to love romantically or as very intimate family (*efrotska* in the lexicon — the deepest, most intimate love)

The Ferrestan theological claim is that God relates to humanity through **every register of affection** — from practical nourishment to intimate devotion. No single word for "love" would suffice. This is a culture that has lexicalized at least six gradations of interpersonal warmth and uses them all to describe the divine relationship.

### 5. Salvation as Recovery and Return, Not Acquittal

The key verb in the soteriology is *kbil* — "to recover, to regain, to return." Salvation is not a legal verdict (acquittal), not a washing (purification), and not a rebirth. It is **returning to where you belong**. The passage ends: *kmum kbil esos lo ot was Annjot, na u swa o loj ot jak* — "will certainly return to the pasture of God's love, which is the pasture of life." The movement is spatial: from wandering (*sbary*), through being sought (*fristo*) and rescued (*bliran*), to returning (*kbil*) to the pasture (*lo*). The entire soteriology is mapped onto **the transhumant cycle**: animals that have strayed from the summer pasture are sought by the herder, saved from danger, and brought back to the territory that sustains them.

### 6. Faith Described as a Three-Part Relationship

Belief is not a single act but three: *isu riwaj isu dokol isu sot Jeswe* — "one who knows, trusts, and follows Jeswe." The three participial verbs are:

- *riwaj* (from *irwaj* "to know somebody, to be familiar with them") — personal knowledge, not abstract belief
- *dokol* — trust (the same word used in the Ferrestan legal system for the trust that *bfolo* violates)
- *sot* — to follow (physical following, as in following a trail or a leader)

Faith in the Ferrestan rendering is: knowing someone personally + trusting them (in the strong legal/communal sense) + physically following their path. This is not creedal assent. It is the kind of loyalty a clan member gives to a trusted *arna* — you know who they are, you trust them with your life, and you follow where they go.

### 7. The Great Commission Recast as a Seasonal Urgency

The commission (section [6]) is rendered with a temporal urgency: *ikwu sjotam es dojna mrojd* — "before they are lost, to the flavor/taste of them." The word *dojna* ("flavor") and *bretmej* ("season") cast the task of spreading the message in terms of **seasonal harvest**: the experiences and seasons of life have a flavor that will be lost if not tasted in time. The imperative is not "go convert" but something closer to "go share [this] with the young, among all peoples, while life's seasons still have their taste." This is a culture that thinks in agricultural and pastoral cycles, where timing is everything and what is not harvested in season is lost.

### 8. The Closing: Love Defined as Self-Sacrifice

The final sentence — *was iskmu eswe os kalbo u na* — "love is made of (*eswe*) self-sacrifice (*kalbo*)" — uses the verb *kalbwo/kalbo* ("to do something for the good of another, self-sacrifice"). This is not a generic statement. The word *eswe* (confirmed as "to be formed out of; substance, material") makes love a **substance** whose material composition is self-sacrifice. Love is not merely accompanied by sacrifice; love *is made of* sacrifice, the way bread is made of flour. In a culture where communal grain stores, shared rite-of-passage danger, and 30-year mountain insurgencies are part of living memory, this is not theology. It is autobiography.
