# Translation of Passage — Revised with Confirmed Lexicon

## Source Text

> Jeswos un kmit o jekto ", skjo dajkê, a njet majnsers ktats sinatlo kwom u na?"
>
> Kjan u it ", kno du was Jawjos Anni doms kolkmat mandoms sajtô mandoms dlirsko. Je wu jims man mejns ktats sinatlo. Wins ihu. Kno du was o lwosk musk, skantiwe seucisu was kno u na. Biswam u jik bjats nejt wins na."
>
> Re jestam fu bfolo stu jomad os stjeki Annjot. Kmum face jom, jemu alkisuj sbari os lo ot was Annjot.

## Sentence-by-Sentence Analysis

### Sentence 1

> **Jeswos un kmit o jekto ", skjo dajkê, a njet majnsers ktats sinatlo kwom u na?"**

| Word | Analysis |
|---|---|
| *Jeswos* | Personal name |
| *un* | Verb particle: past tense simple |
| *kmit* | CONFIRMED: "to ask" |
| *o* | Def. sg. animate nominative article |
| *jekto* | Still unattested. Animate noun — "teacher" or "elder" or "expert." Possibly related to *ekto* "crowd, faction" |
| *skjo* | CONFIRMED: *skj-* vocative + *o* (def. sg. anim.) |
| *dajkê* | "wise one" (*daj* + agentive *-ê*) |
| *a* | Def. sg. inanimate nominative article |
| *njet* | CONFIRMED: "instruction, directive" |
| *majnsers* | *mejns* "prominent, important" + *-(e)rs* (superlative suffix) = "most important" |
| *ktats* | *kt-* "in/within" + *ats* (def. sg. inan. acc.) = "in the" |
| *sinatlo* | CONFIRMED: "The Law" |
| *kwom* | CONFIRMED: question marking particle |
| *u* | Present tense verb particle |
| *na* | "what" |

**Translation:**

> **Jeswos asked the teacher: "O wise one, what is the most important directive in the Law?"**

### Sentence 2

> **Kjan u it ", kno du was Jawjos Anni doms kolkmat mandoms sajtô mandoms dlirsko.**

| Word | Analysis |
|---|---|
| *Kjan u it* | "He speaks" — 3sg anim. ergative + present + "to speak" |
| *kno* | "you (all)" — 2nd pl. ergative |
| *du* | "must" — *d-* + *u* present |
| *was* | "to love, to care for, to nourish" |
| *Jawjos* | Liturgical name or title for God (still unattested; used alongside *Anni*) |
| *Anni* | "God" |
| *doms* | *d-* "with/using" + *oms* (def. sg. anim. comitative) = "with [your whole]" |
| *kolkmat* | *kol* "feeling, emotion" + *kmat* "belly" = the emotional core, the inner being |
| *mandoms* | *man* "and" + *doms* = "and with" |
| *sajtô* | *sajto* "to reason" + circumflex = the reasoning faculty, "mind" |
| *mandoms* | "and with" |
| *dlirsko* | CONFIRMED: "humanness, one's humanity, the human spirit" |

**Translation:** "He spoke: 'You must love Jawjos God with your [whole] heart, and with your mind, and with your human spirit.'"

The Ferrestan triad is **heart** (*kolkmat*), **mind** (*sajtô*), and **human spirit** (*dlirsko*). Where Hebrew/Greek sources have "heart, soul, and strength" or "heart, soul, and mind," the Ferrestan rendering replaces "strength" with *dlirsko* — one's very humanness. The implication: loving God requires the engagement of what makes you distinctly human.

> **Je wu jims man mejns ktats sinatlo.**

**"This is the first and most important [directive] in the Law."**

- *wu* — still unattested; functions as copula "is"
- *jims* — still unattested; "first" (probably *ji* "one" + ordinal suffix)
- *mejns* — CONFIRMED: "prominent, important"

---

> **Wins ihu.**

**"The second is like it."**

- *wins* — still unattested but clear: *wine* "two" + *-s* = "the second"
- *ihu* — *ih-* "as, like" + *u* present = "is like [it]"

---

> **Kno du was o lwosk musk, skantiwe seucisu was kno u na.**

**"You must love your neighbor as you love yourselves."**

- *lwosk* — CONFIRMED: "neighbor"
- *skantiwe* — *skanti* (2nd pl. absolutive) + *-we* (imperative suffix) = reflexive imperative
- *seucisu* — *seuc-* CONFIRMED "in the same way" + *is-* (participial) + *u* (present)

---

> **Biswam u jik bjats nejt wins na."**

**"On these two all the directives stand."**

- *biswam* — still unattested; "on/upon these [two]"
- *bjats* — still unattested; "all of, the whole of"
- *nejt* — likely a variant or collective of *njet* "instruction, directive"

### Sentence 3

> **Re jestam fu bfolo stu jomad os stjeki Annjot.**

| Word | Analysis |
|---|---|
| *Re* | Still unattested as standalone — "for/because." Possibly the free form of the *r-* prefix |
| *jestam* | *j-* + *estam* "all" = "everyone" |
| *fu* | Perfective present: *f-* + *u* = "has [done]" |
| *bfolo* | "a crime, a breach of boundaries" |
| *stu* | Negation particle |
| *jomad* | Likely a reduced form of *ojmad* "to attain, to achieve, to reach" |
| *os* | Def. sg. animate accusative |
| *stjeki* | CONFIRMED: "perfection, flawlessness" |
| *Annjot* | *Anni* "God" + *-ot* (def. sg. anim. genitive, with *j* glide) = "of God" |

**Translation:** "For everyone has transgressed and does not attain the perfection of God."

This is **Romans 3:23**. Where English has "all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God," Ferrestan says *fu bfolo* — "has committed a boundary-breach" — and *stu jomad os stjeki Annjot* — "does not reach the flawlessness of God." The translator chose *stjeki* (perfection/flawlessness) over *anklike* (glorious/brilliant), which was also available in the lexicon. This is a deliberate theological choice: what humans fail to attain is not God's radiance but God's **unblemished completeness**. And sin is not a moral stain but a *bfolo* — a breach of boundaries, the same word used for crimes in the Ferrestan legal system. The Ferrestan concept of sin is structural, not emotional: you have crossed a line you should not have crossed.

---

> **Kmum face jom, jemu alkisuj sbari os lo ot was Annjot.**

| Word | Analysis |
|---|---|
| *Kmum* | *km-* "certainly" + *um* (future) = "will certainly" |
| *face* | "to die" |
| *jom* | "we" (absolutive) |
| *jemu* | "we" (ergative) — "we who..." |
| *alkisuj* | *alk-* "from" + *is-* (participial) + *uj* (present continuous) = "being continuously away from" |
| *sbari* | CONFIRMED: "to wander aimlessly or pointlessly" |
| *os* | Def. sg. animate dative/accusative |
| *lo* | "pasture, hunting ground, field, or fishery owned by an individual or clan" |
| *ot* | Def. sg. animate genitive: "of the" |
| *was* | "love, care, nourishment" |
| *Annjot* | "of God" |

**Translation:** "We will certainly die — we who are wandering aimlessly from the pasture of God's love."

**This is the most culturally revealing line in the passage.** The confirmed meaning of *sbari* — "to wander aimlessly or pointlessly" — transforms the theology completely. The Ferrestan rendering does not say we are "separated from" God (a passive state imposed from outside). It says we are *wandering aimlessly away from* God's pasture — an active, ongoing, purposeless drifting by agents who have lost their way. The continuous participial *alkisuj* (*alk-* "from" + present continuous *-uj*) marks this as a current, unfinished condition.

And the place we are wandering from is *os lo ot was Annjot* — "the pasture of the love of God." The word *lo* does not mean "place" in the abstract. It means the specific pasture, hunting ground, field, or fishery **owned or claimed by a particular individual or clan**. This is the word for the land that feeds you, the territory that your survival depends on. In a transhumant herding culture, an animal that has wandered from *lo* is an animal that will starve or be killed by predators. The Ferrestan image of spiritual death is not a courtroom verdict — it is a **stray animal that has wandered from its herd's pasture and cannot find its way back**.

The colloquial meaning of *sbari* — "to go for a leisurely stroll or hike" — adds an additional layer of irony or pathos: what feels like a casual, harmless amble is actually a journey away from everything that sustains life. The wandering does not feel dangerous. It feels like freedom. But it leads to death.

## Full Revised Translation

> **Jeswos asked the teacher: "O wise one, what is the most important directive in the Law?"**
>
> **He spoke: "You must love Jawjos God with your heart, and with your mind, and with your human spirit. This is the first and most important in the Law. The second is like it: you must love your neighbor as you love yourselves. On these two all the directives stand."**
>
> **For everyone has breached the boundaries and does not attain the perfection of God. We will certainly die — we who are wandering aimlessly from the pasture of God's love.**
